# V1 research contract — revision 0.1

Date: 2026-11-01. This checkpoint operationalizes the supplied handoff and records the methodological corrections proposed in the assessment. These corrections narrow claims and do not expand the source scope.

## Fixed question and scope

Analyze consequences and limits of explicitly documented interpretations of selected AI-governance principles. Primary scope: §§97–111 of the English Vatican edition. Chapter Two foundations require individual paragraph selection before use; none are imported by this checkpoint. One later passage may inform the third case only after its provenance is recorded; it cannot become an unannounced core premise.

## Completion contract

- Source and interpretation ledgers with provenance for every substantive premise.
- Target 25–35 selected source claims; current selection: 28. A selected claim need not become an independent axiom.
- At most 30 principal domain definitions, excluding bookkeeping and generated declarations.
- Target 10–15 substantive positive results and 5–8 countermodel/non-implication results. Never inflate counts with repeated projections, unused hypotheses, or equivalent statements.
- Every fixed target receives a disposition: proved, extra premise needed, countermodel, or source ambiguity. If fewer positive targets survive, document the shortfall and revise the publication claim; never manufacture theorems to meet a quota.
- Exactly three case studies: employment; public benefits/services; bounded high-stakes autonomy.
- Explicit finite satisfaction witness for each theory actually used; include a high-impact decision and an affected person. Every countermodel must name the theory or fragment it satisfies.
- Zero proof placeholders; no hidden custom axioms; theorem-level dependency audits; clean reproducible build and green CI.
- Companion paper of 12–20 pages and a tagged V1.0 release.
- A separate public report of roughly 4–6 pages, beginning with a one-page summary. The reader must not need Lean, logic notation, or this conversation. This is a required completion item, not optional outreach.
- Each public result explains the question, a concrete example, the finding, the assumptions, the practical significance, and what has not been established. Distinguish hypothetical examples from measured real-world evidence. Maintain this explanation alongside the technical result.
- Target 2,000–5,000 Lean lines; scope audit at 4,000 and pause at 5,000 if the agreed result suite is incomplete.

After V1, expansion requires a separate V2 contract. The numerical targets are scope controls, not evidence of significance.

## Mandatory semantic rules

1. Requirements and actual provision use separate relations. An obligation never establishes its own fulfillment.
2. Models may represent violations without inconsistency. Compliance means satisfying the chosen requirements for a named scope.
3. Normative rules are explicit fields or hypotheses, never concealed in neutral domain definitions.
4. Derived results retain their source-premise IDs and interpretation IDs.
5. Empirical and anthropological source positions are distinguished from normative policy rules.
6. Minimal and stronger theories retain a refinement map. Robustness beyond ordinary inheritance is tested across alternate, potentially incomparable interpretations.
7. A theorem proving a direct premise projection is a useful interface lemma, but is not a flagship research result.
8. A finite countermodel refutes a precisely quantified implication in a named semantics. It does not establish that a real institution behaves that way.

## Prototype decision gate

Proceed to the full V1 only after the first theorem, countermodel, and non-vacuous satisfying model. At least two of the following must be supported by concrete evidence: useful dependency exposed; defensible mapping maintained; interpretive ambiguity clarified; publicly explainable failure revealed; reusable modeling method demonstrated.
