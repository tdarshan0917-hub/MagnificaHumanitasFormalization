# What this build establishes

Travis Darshan · Local Law 144 case study · Build 0.2 · 5 November 2026

We have moved from one pilot to five employer document reviews. Each review asks what a candidate can learn from the employer's published material: who handles the process, how to request review, and what is described about making a correction. These are public-document findings, not tests of an employer's internal operation.

## What we found

| Employer | Useful published provision | Question that remains |
|---|---|---|
| Citizens | Candidate assistance and recruiter review. | The reviewed set does not describe an NYC outcome-reconsideration route. This does not establish that no route exists. |
| Snowflake | Conditional rights to contest AI candidacy decisions and request human review. | The material does not establish when those conditions give an NYC candidate access. |
| Instacart | A CodeSignal result can be referred to a human with authority to change the decision. | This provision cannot be generalized to every listed tool, and we have not observed a correction being applied. |
| NBCUniversal | Its March 2026 policy describes requesting further human review of recruitment decisions. | The documents do not explain the full route from a review finding to an applied correction and confirmation. |
| SharkNinja | Human override before screening, profiling objections and requests for review without AI outputs. | The timing of post-decision reconsideration is unclear. Its NYC notice still describes a prospective August 2026 launch, so present use is unresolved. |

Evidence: [source register](data/sources.json), [section-linked classifications](data/employers.json), and the five individual sheets in [employers](employers/). Each statement has a tool, population, stage and time boundary. Published historical or conditional provisions remain labeled that way.

## Why this helps an institution

Consider a hypothetical candidate who disputes an assessment score. A company may already publish a human-review route. The next questions are practical: can that reviewer change the relevant decision, does the finding reach the team that must act, and does the candidate receive confirmation? A published promise answers only the questions it actually addresses.

The review kit turns these questions into requests for evidence: a case-owner record, challenge instructions, reviewer authority, a receipt for the finding, a reassessment record and confirmation to the candidate. An institution could use those records to identify which part of its procedure needs attention. We have not yet demonstrated improvement at an institution; that would require an authorized test of an actual workflow.

## What changed in our method

The first five reviews required sharper coding. We now separate review before a decision from a challenge afterward; an assessment-result challenge from a final rejection appeal; and one tool's protection from coverage of all tools. Data-correction rights and alternative assessment requests stay separate. This prevents both overstating safeguards and incorrectly declaring them absent.

The three original discovery queries yielded four eligible groups including the pilot. A fourth query supplied the fifth. The protocol discloses that the query ran before the written amendment, and that the search archive is grouped rather than a complete ranked URL record. The sample is exploratory, not representative.

All five records were reconciled by the same assistant against the reviewed sources. This is a second reading, not an independent-coder study. Original source bytes have not been archived. Final publication requires source-version rechecking and a clearer discovery archive.
