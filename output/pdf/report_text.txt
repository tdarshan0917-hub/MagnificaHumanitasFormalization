The consolidated finding

Wider appeal access and correction capacity are separate requirements in our tested policies. A case can satisfy either while failing the other. Lean checked the exact evidence needed to satisfy all three current safeguards policies together.

A rejected, harmed applicant has a named officer and can appeal, but the desk cannot correct the decision. Both appeal policies pass; the correction-capacity policy fails. Before exclusion, another affected applicant with no stipulated harm lacks appeal: the correction-capacity policy passes, but broader appeal fails.

PARTICIPATION, DISTRIBUTED CONTROL AND LOCAL ACTION

Who can actually<br/>act?

Sharing control between institutions need not give a community an effective action of its own.

Earlier: influence and authority

Our earlier community recommendation is recorded, amendable and sometimes changes the review outcome, yet an institutional override can still decide. A binding rule supplies final control. Separately, a technically capable operator may lack institutional recognition. Neither influence nor recognition alone establishes a justified right to govern.

Now: two controllers and a local pause

An operator and an independent review board each have a separate hold input that can pause execution of the disputed benefits denial. Neither can cancel the other's hold. The community can submit and amend a pause request, but the first evaluator ignores it. The repair makes that local request effective alongside both external holds.

Power in this pause protocol | Initial rule | Local input enabled

Operator can cause a pause? | Yes | Yes

Board can cause a pause? | Yes | Yes

Community can cause a pause? | No | Yes

No holds or local request: can resume? | Yes | Yes

What Lean checked

Initially, changing the local request has no pause effect under any external setting. Enabling it gives the community a pause even when both external controllers release their holds. Both external powers remain effective. The original named assignment, appeal and correction-capacity safeguards stay fixed; a pause itself does not award the benefit or correct the record.

Assumptions and practical use

This hypothetical protocol assumes requests reach the evaluator and every asserted hold is honored. Independence is stipulated. Paragraphs 108-109 motivate community action but do not specify this rule. The test concerns temporary suspension, not final award authority or complete subsidiarity. Ask which action the community can actually cause, alongside how many institutions share control.

PUBLIC BENEFITS / OBJECTIONS AND RULE CORRECTION

Does an objection<br/>change the rule?

A recorded objection becomes effective only when it reaches the rule actually used to calculate awards.

The existing case

Two applications each have verified eligibility score 60 and cutoff 50. The first rule deducts 15 points for assisted intake, producing a denial, while online intake receives a grant. Our adopted policy says that channel difference must not affect entitlement. Both calculations are truthfully explained.

Three responses to the objection

A request to remove the penalty is recorded. We compare the assigned design reviewer without rule-changing power, the deployment actor who handles individual denials, and the assigned design reviewer with explicit criteria-revision power. A computed check also tests the proposed awards against the adopted eligibility rule.

Who acts on the recorded request? | Rule changes? | Assisted application

Design reviewer without criteria power | No | 45: deny

Denial handler without design assignment | No | 45: deny

Assigned, empowered design reviewer; valid proposal | Yes | 60: grant

What Lean checked

The corrected selector actually feeds the scorer. Both applications then compute as grants. Scores, cutoff, assignment, appeal and correction channels remain fixed. The check rejects proposals that would restore the penalty; the corrected rule survives every finite sequence of the modeled revision attempts.

The practical question and assumptions

Ask who can change the operative criteria, alongside whether an objection was logged. This hypothetical process assumes supplied role evidence and perfect enforcement of the checks, and considers only our two candidate rules. Recomputing an award does not automatically reopen an old denial or deliver payment. The separate appeal-to-record-correction path is shown on page 5.

Earlier disclosure result: a notice with score 47, cutoff 50 and published safeguards can still name a service contact rather than the actual assignee. NEW RESULT / HIGH-STAKES AUTONOMY

A stop that arrives too late

The same final checklist can hide a crucial difference: whether control reached the system before the harm occurred.

The example

We model a hazardous autonomous action. A stop request is sent, delivered, and followed by a corrected decision record. One run delivers the stop before execution; the other delivers it afterward. Both finish with the stop recorded as delivered and the administrative record corrected. Only the timely run avoids physical loss.

This is a hypothetical case with explicit rules. We assume a delivered stop is obeyed and that none of the five available actions can reverse the physical loss. Those assumptions are not discoveries about real systems.

Check | Timely run | Late run

Stop requested? | Yes | Yes

Stop delivered? | Before execution | After execution

Record corrected afterward? | Yes | Yes

Record-correction officer identifiable? | Yes | Yes

Physical loss in the model? | No | Yes

What Lean checked

The two runs contain the same four events; only the delivery and execution order changes. Lean checked the different outcomes. It also checked that once physical loss occurs, no finite sequence of the available actions removes it. Correcting the record remains possible, but it does not restore the physical outcome.

There is a positive result too: if the stop has already arrived while the state is loss-free, every continuation allowed by these rules stays loss-free. That is a conditional guarantee inside our model, not a proof that real operators always detect danger or respond in time.

The practical lesson

Ask when control became effective, not just whether a human was involved. Also ask what a promised correction can change: the record, the decision, or the physical harm. Those protections are different.

Where the limits matter

The result depends on the stated irreversibility and stop rules. It does not establish that every real loss is permanent or that the encyclical uniquely requires this particular mechanism. We have initial examples in all three agreed case domains; the full research project remains unfinished.

APPEAL, REVIEW AND IMPLEMENTED CORRECTION

When does an appeal<br/>change the decision?

An available channel, a review finding and an applied update are different steps.

The already-issued denial

The assisted-intake denial is already stored. The scoring rule has now been corrected, so reassessment yields a grant. An independent board can review an admitted appeal; a separate case office has power to amend the record. We check what happens between those capacities and the actual update.

What happens in the modeled path? | Stored decision

Appeal reviewed, but no handoff to the correction office | Denial remains

Review completed, but no update applied | Denial remains

Update attempted before completed review | Denial remains

Appeal admitted, reviewed, handed off and update applied | Reassessed grant

What Lean checked

The complete path needs open admission, an actual request, completed review, a working handoff, correction power and an update addressed to the correction office. Having the powers available does not perform those actions. The board has a usable intervention path exactly when access, power and the handoff exist. Adding the handoff preserves submission and review behavior.

How this fits the earlier appeal finding

Both tested appeal readings reject exclusion without accessible appeal. The broader reading also requires appeal for affected people in high-impact decisions before exclusion; the narrower one can accept that earlier stage without appeal. Internal human review alone does not give the person access. The new path examines what happens after an appeal is actually admitted.

Assumptions and practical use

The reassessment uses the corrected rule already checked in the previous build. The hypothetical workflow enforces the shown order and uses authentic findings from that composed path. The result changes the stored award, not payment or full restoration of every harm. Ask where a reviewed finding goes and which action actually changes the record.

NEW RESULT / INDEPENDENT OVERSIGHT

Does the board cover<br/>your decision?

An independent board can exist without being able to oversee a particular benefit denial.

The example

Return to our hypothetical public-benefits office. Internal staff review a denial. An independent board can review routine inquiries, but the denial falls outside its remit. The person still has a named officer, an appeal and correction capacity. Those safeguards do not give the board jurisdiction over the denial.

Check for the benefit denial | Outside remit | Review only | Remit + power

Independent board exists and can review? | Yes | Yes | Yes

Denial within the board's remit? | No | Yes | Yes

Board has intervention power for denial? | Yes | No | Yes

Meets our actionable-oversight test? | No | No | Yes

In the first version, intervention power alone cannot overcome the missing remit. Internal review and the person's appeal and correction access stay fixed across all three versions.

What Lean checked

The first version has a real oversight role for routine inquiries but no independent coverage of the denial. The second covers the denial but lacks intervention power. The third supplies both. Each missing provision can be repaired while the other safeguards stay fixed. Both narrow and broad appeal checklists pass throughout; the oversight gap remains separate.

The practical question

When told 'we have independent oversight,' ask which decisions it covers and what the reviewer can do about them. The existence of a board, the right to review and the power to intervene are separate checks.

Assumptions and limits

Independence and powers are stipulated in this hypothetical example. Our test checks capacity, not actual action, speed or successful correction. The source's call for effective oversight is broader: criteria and community participation also matter. This example does not settle those requirements or prove full source compliance. The exact board arrangement is our interpretation of paragraphs 106 and 108.
