# MAGNIFICA HUMANITAS FORMALIZATION
## Research handoff, bounded V1 contract, and execution plan

### 0. Project identity

Working title:

**Magnifica Humanitas Formalization: A Machine-Checked AI Governance Core in Lean 4**

Primary source:

Pope Leo XIV, *Magnifica Humanitas: On Safeguarding the Human Person in the Time of Artificial Intelligence*, signed May 15, 2026.

The source document is an encyclical concerned with Catholic social doctrine, technological power, artificial intelligence, human dignity, responsibility, governance, transparency, subsidiarity, justice, work, freedom, and related questions.

For V1, the project is **not** an attempt to formalize the entire encyclical.

The formal source lock is:

**Primary AI scope: paragraphs 97–111.**

Earlier Chapter Two material may be used only to define the foundational concepts explicitly invoked by the AI section:

- human dignity
- common good
- universal destination of goods
- subsidiarity
- solidarity
- social justice

No additional chapter becomes part of V1 merely because it is interesting.

Later paragraphs may be consulted as interpretive evidence or used in one bounded case study, but they do not expand the formal core.

---

# 1. Central research question

The project is built around one precise question:

**What governance obligations follow mechanically from a carefully documented formal interpretation of the AI principles in *Magnifica Humanitas*, and which apparently plausible governance conclusions do not follow without additional assumptions?**

This is critical.

The project is not:

> “Can Lean prove the Pope is correct?”

It is:

> “If we state the encyclical's relevant premises precisely, what follows from them, what does not follow from them, and where are additional assumptions required?”

That distinction must remain visible throughout the repository.

---

# 2. Epistemic boundary

Lean can certify valid consequences of encoded assumptions.

Lean cannot by itself establish:

- the truth of Catholic theology;
- the existence of God;
- the metaphysical truth of inherent human dignity;
- the empirical accuracy of every factual claim about current AI;
- that one English-language reading of an ambiguous paragraph is the uniquely correct interpretation;
- that a normative premise is universally binding merely because it occurs in the source.

Therefore every source-derived proposition must receive an explicit epistemic classification.

V1 claim categories:

```text
TheologicalPremise
AnthropologicalPremise
EmpiricalClaim
NormativePrinciple
InstitutionalRequirement
DerivedClaim
InterpretiveChoice
```

No derived theorem may hide a normative or theological premise inside a definition merely to make the theorem easier to prove.

V1 is FINISHED when all of the following exist:

- source scope frozen to §§97–111 plus selected Chapter Two foundations;
- 25–35 accepted source-linked principles;
- no more than 30 principal domain definitions;
- 10–15 substantive positive theorems;
- 5–8 countermodels, independence results, or non-implication results;
- 3 concrete AI-system case studies;

- zero `sorry`;

No new chapter is added to V1.

Any expansion becomes **V2** and requires a new written research contract.

# 7. Normative modeling strategy

V1 should NOT begin with a full custom deontic-modal logic.

Use ordinary Lean propositions and predicates first.

Example style:

```lean
def AccountableDecision (d : Decision) : Prop :=
  ∃ a : HumanActor,
    ResponsibleFor a d ∧
    Identifiable a
```

and:

```lean
def MeaningfulRecourse (p : Person) (d : Decision) : Prop :=
  CanChallenge p d ∧ CanRemedy p d
```

This makes the semantic commitments transparent.

Only after V1 should we consider a formal deontic layer such as:

```text
O φ   obligatory
P φ   permitted
F φ   forbidden
```

A Phase II could embed a genuine modal/deontic semantics if V1 demonstrates that it would add analytical value.

# 8. Initial source principles to investigate

Do not immediately declare all of these axioms.

They are the candidate pool from which the final 25–35 source principles will be selected.

## Human primacy

Candidate interpretation:

- AI is a tool rather than a human moral agent.
- AI lacks moral responsibility in the relevant sense.
- human conscience and freedom must remain authoritative in determining AI use and limits.

## Consequential decision-making

Candidate interpretation:

- some AI-mediated decisions affect rights, opportunity, status, and freedom;
- such decisions require heightened governance;
- automated neutrality cannot simply be presumed.

## Responsibility

Candidate interpretation:

- consequential decisions require identifiable responsibility;
- responsibility cannot terminate in the artificial system;
- institutional deployment does not eliminate human responsibility.

## Review and recourse

Candidate interpretation:

- affected persons must have meaningful ways to challenge consequential decisions;
- governance must support correction and remedy when harm occurs.

## Oversight

Candidate interpretation:

- high-impact uses may require independent oversight;
- ethics statements alone are insufficient governance.

## Participation

Candidate interpretation:

- affected communities are not merely passive objects of technical governance;
- their participation has normative significance.

## Transparency

Candidate interpretation:

- relevant systems and institutions have transparency obligations;
- transparency does not necessarily mean full technical interpretability.

## Developer responsibility

Candidate interpretation:

- developers bear responsibility for design choices;
- technical design embeds or operationalizes assumptions about persons and society.

## Subsidiarity

Candidate interpretation:

- higher-level technological governance should not unnecessarily displace the agency of lower-level persons and communities;
- centralization requires justification where local participation is possible.

These interpretations must be validated against the source before they enter the formal theory.

# 9. Fixed V1 positive theorem targets

These are theorem TARGETS, not assumed truths.

The first research objective is to determine which are actually derivable from the final source principles.

## T1 — Human Responsibility

Tentative statement:

```lean
theorem highImpact_has_human_responsibility
    (d : Decision)
    (hImpact : HighImpact d)
    (hComp : MHCompliant d) :
    ∃ a : HumanActor, ResponsibleFor a d
```

Research question:

Does the source actually justify this directly, or do we need an explicit responsibility principle?

## T2 — No AI as Terminal Responsibility Bearer

Flagship candidate.

# 10. Mandatory countermodel / non-implication program

This is one of the most important parts of the project.

Formalizing only positive consequences would make this a transcription exercise.

We need systems satisfying one attractive principle while violating another.

## C1 — Transparency does not imply accountability

Construct:

```text
Transparent s
¬ Accountable s
```

If consistent with the base theory, prove:

```lean
¬ (∀ s, Transparent s → Accountable s)
```

or produce a finite model/witness.

---

## C2 — Human oversight does not imply recourse

A human can review a decision without affected persons having a meaningful appeal.

Test formally.

---

## C3 — Responsibility does not imply remedy

An identifiable responsible actor may exist while no remedy process exists.

---

## C4 — Explainability does not imply justice

A discriminatory system could be perfectly explainable.

---

## C5 — Decentralization does not imply subsidiarity

Merely distributing technical control may not satisfy the normative idea of subsidiarity.

---

## C6 — Participation does not automatically imply authority

Consultation and decision-making power are distinct.

---

## C7/C8 reserve

Potentially:

- oversight without independence;
- accountability without transparency.

A failed implication is considered a successful research result if the formal assumptions and countermodel are clear.

# 11. Three fixed case studies

V1 gets exactly three.

## Case A — Employment decision system

Scenario:

AI ranks applicants for employment.

Questions:

- Is the decision high-impact?
- Who bears responsibility?
- What review exists?
- What recourse exists?
- Does explanation suffice?
- What happens if ranking errors are discovered?

---

## Case B — Public-benefit / public-service decision system

Scenario:

AI recommends or determines access to a public benefit or public service.

Questions:

- rights/opportunity impact;
- public authority responsibility;
- human review;
- appeal;
- remedy;
- transparency;
- affected-person participation.

This is especially useful because the encyclical explicitly identifies access to public services among consequential uses.

---

## Case C — High-stakes autonomous decision system

Preferably a bounded lethal/autonomous-weapons example or similarly extreme governance system.

This case should stress-test:

- terminal human responsibility;
- effective human control;
- reviewability;
- irreversibility;
- whether after-the-fact recourse is meaningful.

Do not let this domain expand the entire project into military ethics.

It is one stress test only.

# 12. Interpretation ledger

Natural-language normative texts are not formal specifications.

Therefore the repository must explicitly preserve interpretive choices.

Example:

```text
I-004

Source:
MH §105

Phrase:
"responsibility must be clearly defined"

Formal reading A:
Every high-impact decision has at least one identifiable responsible human actor.

Formal reading B:
Every high-impact decision has at least one identifiable responsible institutional actor.

Status:
Both admissible.

Question:
Which downstream theorems survive both readings?
```

This creates one of the project's potentially strongest contributions:

**interpretation-robust theorems.**

Definition:

A theorem is interpretation-robust when it holds across every admissible formalization in the project's interpretation family.

This could become a flagship concept.

---

# 13. Minimal and strong interpretations

Eventually maintain two theory instances:

```text
MHMinimal
MHStrong
```

`MHMinimal` includes only implications with very conservative textual support.

`MHStrong` includes defensible but stronger interpretations.

Then ask:

```text
Which V1 theorems hold in both?
Which require MHStrong?
```

This is substantially more intellectually honest than pretending there is one inevitable formalization of the encyclical.

# 16. Technical strategy

Preferred baseline:

Lean 4 + pinned Mathlib.

Do not initially depend on a large modal/deontic framework unless necessary.

However, relevant prior infrastructure exists.

The Lean project `FormalizedFormalLogic/Foundation` formalizes substantial propositional, first-order, intuitionistic, modal/provability, and semantic machinery in Lean 4, so a later modal/deontic extension would not need to begin from nothing.

A neighboring research methodology is LogiKEy, which uses higher-order logic and semantic embeddings to engineer deontic logics and ethical/legal domain theories for intelligent autonomous systems.

Those projects demonstrate that formal ethical/normative reasoning is a real research area.

Our intended contribution is different:

**source-linked formalization of a contemporary AI-governance encyclical in Lean, preserving assumptions and interpretations while proving consequences and countermodels.**

Do not claim priority until a much deeper literature search has been completed.

# 18. What makes this a meaningful project rather than a novelty

The project succeeds only if it produces at least one of these:

### Result A — non-obvious dependency

Example:

Meaningful recourse requires more than identifiable responsibility.

### Result B — non-implication

Example:

Transparency does not imply accountability.

### Result C — interpretive robustness

Example:

A human-responsibility theorem survives both minimal and strong readings of the source.

### Result D — inconsistency/tension

Example:

Two plausible governance principles require an additional reconciliation principle when instantiated in a particular case.

### Result E — reusable methodology

A claim/source/interpretation/theorem framework that can be applied to:

- EU AI Act;
- UNESCO AI ethics recommendations;
- NIST AI RMF;
- other religious/social documents;
- corporate AI constitutions or governance frameworks.

If V1 only converts English sentences into Lean predicates and proves tautologies, the project has failed intellectually even if the code compiles.

# 21. Research-integrity rules

These rules are mandatory.

1. Never say Lean proved Catholic doctrine true.
2. Never disguise normative premises as definitions.
3. Every substantive source premise receives a paragraph reference.
4. Every interpretive strengthening is logged.
5. Counterexamples are retained, not engineered away.
6. Failed theorem targets are documented.
7. No conclusion stronger than its assumptions.
8. Empirical claims are separated from normative claims.
9. Source meaning and formal abstraction are distinguished.
10. V1 scope cannot expand without a written V2 contract.
